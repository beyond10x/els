On the night of 4 October 2026, between 00:56 and 04:30 UTC, an operator, a design session, an
orchestrator session and its sub-agents took a run of design decisions and shipped several product
sites. Read afterwards, almost every piece of that work was a case under a protocol of the kind ELS
declares: a question became a decision only once it was recorded, a change merged only after its
checks passed on the revision being merged, a red check was repaired before anything else moved,
and a site was published only from a merged, verified commit.

The work falls into four protocols, named the way ELS names them:

| Protocol | What a case of it is |
|---|---|
| `design.decision/1` | A question taken to a recorded decision: analysis, the operator's decision, a draft, the record. |
| `software.change/1` | A change implemented, proven on the current revision by its checks, reviewed and merged. |
| `ci.repair/1` | A failing check investigated and repaired, interrupting whatever the session was doing. |
| `release.source/1` | A merged commit published, and the published result verified against that commit. |

ELS now ships `software.change/1`; the other three are named here for what the night shows and
are not ELS protocols. See [Protocols](./protocols/index.md) for what is shipped.

## Where the data comes from

The data is a hand-compiled session record: message and tool-call timestamps from the agent host's
session record, and pull request opened and merged times. It is not the output of a shipped tool.
The Metaharness session miner (decision record 0088) is meant to produce this record in future.
Participants are named by role: the operator is `human:operator`, and the orchestrating session is
the orchestrator session. The record itself is published beside the replay as
[`data.json`](pathname:///showcase/2026-10-04/data.json).
